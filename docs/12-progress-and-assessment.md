# 12 — Progress and Assessment

## Purpose
Define measurable learning progress without reducing language ability to a single misleading score.

## Dimensions
Track grammar accuracy, vocabulary knowledge/retrieval, pronunciation where measurable, fluency, scenario performance, consistency, and goal progress.

## Assessment
Initial assessment establishes a starting estimate. Ongoing assessment uses repeated evidence across contexts. Assessment must remain revisable.

## Mastery
Mastery requires consistent successful performance at an appropriate difficulty level across multiple opportunities.

## Reporting
Learner-facing reports emphasize trends, strengths, current focus, and actionable next steps. Avoid false precision.

## Metrics
Each metric has a definition, evidence source, confidence, and aggregation rule. Product telemetry must not be presented as learning progress.

## Acceptance Criteria
- Progress is multidimensional.
- Scores have defined meaning.
- Repeated evidence influences mastery.
- Reports are understandable and actionable.
- Low-confidence measurements are labeled or excluded.
